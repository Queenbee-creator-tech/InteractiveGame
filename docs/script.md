# Phase 1 — Draft Game Script

> Working draft. “Narrator” is a placeholder until the guide character/name is finalized. Audio must match on-screen captions.

## Scene 1 — Mission Briefing

**Narrator:** “We have a problem. A blood clot is blocking blood flow through an artery in the brain. In an ischemic stroke, restoring blood flow quickly matters. Your mission is to investigate how we might capture this clot and remove it.”

**Prompt:** “Explore the vessel. Select the clot when you’re ready.”

**Clot hover/focus:** “Blood clot — material blocking blood flow through this vessel.”

**Narrator after click:** “Mechanical thrombectomy removes a clot from a blood vessel. One approach uses an aspiration catheter: a small tube that applies suction to the clot.”

## Scene 2 — Retrieval Challenge

**Narrator:** “Try the conventional aspiration catheter.”

**Prompt:** “Activate aspiration.”

**After test:** “Suction can pull the clot toward the catheter, but there is an engineering constraint. In smaller vessels, the catheter must also be small and flexible, and aspiration force decreases with catheter diameter. We need a way to improve how the catheter holds onto the clot without simply making the tube larger.”

**Choice prompt:** “What should we investigate next?”
- Make the catheter enormous.
- Look for a biological structure that grips soft material.
- Ignore the clot and increase speed.

**Feedback:** “A larger catheter is not always practical in small, delicate vessels. Let’s look for a strategy that improves mechanical grip.”

## Scene 3 — Nature Investigation

**Narrator:** “Nature has been solving gripping and retention problems for millions of years. Take a closer look at the teeth of a boa constrictor.”

**Prompt:** “Explore the teeth, then choose the feature that seems most useful.”

**Tooth hover/focus:** “Recurved tooth — curves inward/backward rather than pointing straight out.”

**Narrator:** “Boa teeth are not identical across the jaw. Research connecting tooth shape with strike mechanics found differences in curvature, and during some strikes curved posterior teeth helped ensnare prey. Curved teeth also contribute during swallowing.”

**Choice:** “Which feature matters most for our engineering problem?”
- Their color.
- Their recurved geometry.
- Venom.

**Feedback:** “Right—the useful feature here is the recurved geometry and the retention it provides. Boa constrictors are not the inspiration because of venom.”

## Scene 4 — Mechanism Challenge

**Narrator:** “Now think like a bioinspired designer. We do not need to build something that looks like a snake. We need to abstract what the biological structure does.”

**Prompt:** “What principle should we transfer?”
- Copy the snake’s appearance.
- Directional mechanical retention.
- Add venom to the catheter.

**Correct feedback:** “Exactly. A recurved structure can engage soft material and resist it slipping away in the opposite direction. That function—not the snake’s appearance—is the idea we want.”

**Wrong feedback:** “Look at the function rather than the organism’s appearance. What does the recurved tooth help accomplish?”

## Scene 5 — Engineering Lab

**Narrator:** “Researchers at Purdue translated that principle into a thrombectomy concept called TRAP—the Thrombus Retrieval Aspiration Platform.”

**Prompt:** “Place the gripping feature where it can contact the clot.”

**After placement:** “TRAP places backward-curved microscale structures on the inside of the catheter’s distal tip. When aspiration draws the clot into the opening, those structures can engage its surface and add mechanical grip.”

**Narrator:** “The design was developed by Ángel Enríquez and Hyowon Lee at Purdue, and the technology was licensed to Emboa Medical for further development.”

## Scene 6 — Test the Design

**Narrator:** “Let’s test the concept.”

**Prompt:** “Run the smooth catheter, then run the TRAP concept.”

**After test:** “The important difference is not simply stronger suction. TRAP combines aspiration with mechanical engagement from the recurved microstructures.”

**Narrator:** “Purdue reported greater removal force in bench testing and improved first-attempt performance in a difficult vascular model. But keep the evidence in context: these are preclinical/model results. The device still requires further safety and effectiveness validation before we can treat those results as clinical outcomes.”

## Scene 7 — Bioinspiration Debrief

**Narrator:** “Can you reconstruct the bioinspiration pathway?”

**Prompt:** “Put the five ideas in order.”

**Cards:**
- Recurved boa teeth
- Retain/ensnare prey
- Directional mechanical retention
- Recurved microscale structures inside catheter tip
- Mechanical clot engagement during aspiration

**Success:** “You got it. This is analogical reasoning: identify a biological structure and function, abstract the useful principle, then translate that principle into an engineered solution.”

**Narrator:** “It also shows an important limitation of bioinspiration. Nature gives engineers strategies—not automatically finished medical devices. The engineered design still has manufacturing, safety, and validation constraints.”

## Scene 8 — Mission Complete

**Narrator:** “Mission complete. The key connection is not ‘snake teeth are sharp.’ It is that recurved boa teeth provide a directional retention strategy, and TRAP translates that strategy into recurved microstructures intended to improve clot engagement during aspiration.”

**Final note:** “TRAP is presented according to the preclinical/development evidence available in the sources used for this project.”
