# Final Six-Section Player Script — Working Production Draft

> **Purpose:** Player-facing narration, prompts, feedback, and timing for the six-section game.
>
> **Important:** Rubric notes and source guardrails stay in planning files; they are not shown to the player. Captions must match all spoken narration. Final wording can be tightened during implementation, but scientific meaning must remain unchanged.

## Timing target

The assignment maximum is **5:00**. This draft intentionally leaves room for different player speeds.

- Target narration/audio: about **3:00–3:20**
- Target required interactions/transitions: about **1:00–1:20**
- Expected first play: about **4:00–4:40**
- Optional exploration, Sources, Transcript, and Replay are not required to finish the core mission.

Do not add mandatory dialogue or interactions without re-timing the complete game.

# 1 — Hospital: The Challenge

**WartsWorth:**  
“Welcome, researcher! We have a problem to solve. An ischemic stroke can happen when a blood clot blocks blood flow in the brain. Our challenge is to understand how that clot can be captured and removed.”

**Prompt:**  
“Investigate the vessel.”

**After player examines the clot:**  
“That clot is blocking the pathway. One way doctors can remove a clot is aspiration thrombectomy. *Aspiration* means using suction through a catheter to engage and remove the clot.”

**Prompt:**  
“Try the aspiration catheter.”

**After the demonstration:**  
“Aspiration can retrieve clots, but keeping soft clot material securely engaged can be an engineering challenge. Instead of assuming the answer is simply ‘more suction,’ let’s ask a bioinspiration question: **How might nature grip and retain soft material?**”

**Transition:** WartsWorth changes into field gear.

**WartsWorth:**  
“Field trip! Nature has had a very long time to experiment.”

---

# 2 — Explore: Nature + Biology

**WartsWorth:**  
“Somewhere in this habitat is an organism with a structure that may give us a clue. Look carefully!”

**Prompt:**  
“Search the habitat.”

**When the hidden boa is discovered:**

**WartsWorth:**  
“Whoa! A boa constrictor. Fascinating—and I am suspiciously snack-sized. Let’s observe from here.”

**Prompt:**  
“Use the scanner to investigate the boa’s teeth.”

**WartsWorth:**  
“Boa teeth are recurved—they curve backward—and their shape varies across the jaws. Research on boa feeding has shown that curved teeth can help ensnare and retain prey.”

**Scanner interaction:** player examines the recurved geometry.

**WartsWorth:**  
“Hold still, buddy! We’re borrowing your idea, not your teeth.”

**Scanner takes a digital snapshot.**

**WartsWorth:**  
“That’s an important part of bioinspiration. We study how a biological structure works, then abstract the useful principle instead of copying or taking the organism itself.”

**Prompt:**  
“What should engineers borrow?”

- The snake’s appearance
- Venom
- **Directional mechanical retention**

**Correct feedback:**  
“Exactly. The useful idea is the relationship between the recurved structure and its retention function.”

**Transition:** the digital tooth geometry travels into the lab.

---

# 3 — Design: The Innovation

**WartsWorth:**  
“Lab goggles on! We brought the biological observation with us. Now we have to translate it into engineering.”

**Prompt:**  
“Use the recurved geometry to help solve the clot-retention challenge.”

**Player places/orients the recurved feature inside the catheter tip.**

**WartsWorth:**  
“Nice reasoning. Researchers at Purdue developed a technology called **TRAP—the Thrombus Retrieval Aspiration Platform**. Instead of copying an entire snake, the design uses backward-curved microscale structures inside the distal catheter tip.”

**Close-up animation.**

**WartsWorth:**  
“Aspiration draws the clot toward the opening. The recurved structures can then add mechanical engagement and retention.”

**Side-by-side prompt:**  
“Match the biological structure and function to the engineered structure and function.”

**Correct feedback:**  
“Boa tooth: recurved structure for retention. TRAP: recurved microstructures for clot engagement during aspiration. Same functional logic, very different context.”

**WartsWorth:**  
“The technology was developed through Purdue research and licensed to Emboa Medical for further development. But a clever design still has to be tested.”

---

# 4 — Test: Does It Work?

**WartsWorth:**  
“Time to put the idea to the test. First, observe aspiration with a smooth catheter. Then compare the bioinspired concept.”

**Prompt:**  
“Run both tests.”

**After simulation:**  
“What changed?”

**Player examines the close-up.**

**WartsWorth:**  
“The key difference is the added mechanical interaction. In the bioinspired design, the recurved structures can engage the clot as aspiration draws it into the catheter.”

**Evidence card opens.**

**WartsWorth:**  
“Our sources describe bench and model/preclinical testing. That can tell researchers whether a design is promising, but it is not the same as proving how it will perform in patients.”

**Prompt:**  
“Which conclusion is supported?”

**Correct answer:**  
“The design adds a bioinspired mechanical clot-engagement strategy and has shown promising preclinical/model results, but further validation is needed.”

**Correct feedback:**  
“Exactly. Good science means matching the conclusion to the evidence.”

---

# 5 — Apply: A Healthier Future

**WartsWorth:**  
“An invention doesn’t jump straight from an idea into a hospital. Development happens in steps.”

**Prompt:**  
“Explore the development pathway.”

**Milestones:**  
1. Observe the biological strategy  
2. Develop the TRAP concept at Purdue  
3. Translate/license the technology to Emboa Medical  
4. Test the design in models/preclinical settings  
5. Continue safety and effectiveness validation

**WartsWorth:**  
“This is where constraints and trade-offs matter. A medical device has to do more than copy a useful shape—it has to be engineered, tested, and evaluated for the conditions where it may eventually be used.”

**Prompt:**  
“What problem is this work ultimately trying to address?”

**Correct feedback:**  
“Clot retrieval. The goal is to translate a biological retention strategy into a useful medical-device strategy while continuing to test whether it is safe and effective.”

**WartsWorth:**  
“You’ve followed the idea from nature to engineering. One challenge left!”

---

# 6 — Final Challenge: Make a Difference

**WartsWorth:**  
“Final mission! Rebuild the bioinspiration pathway.”

**Prompt:**  
“Put the five pieces in order.”

1. Recurved/inward-curving boa teeth  
2. Retain/ensnare prey  
3. Directional mechanical retention  
4. Backward-curved microscale structures inside the catheter tip  
5. Added mechanical clot engagement/retention during aspiration

**Success:**  
“You got it! That is the heart of this example: **structure → function → abstracted principle → engineered structure → engineered function.**”

**Final prompt:**  
“Why is TRAP bioinspired?”

**Correct answer:**  
“Engineers studied a biological structure and function, abstracted the useful principle, and translated that principle into an engineered design.”

**WartsWorth:**  
“Exactly! Nature gave us a strategy—not a finished medical device. Observation, analogical reasoning, engineering, testing, and iteration turned that strategy into a technology worth investigating.”

**Mission Complete screen:**  
“Mission Complete!”

Buttons:
- Sources
- Transcript
- Replay
- Review Journey

**Final WartsWorth line:**  
“Small creatures. Big solutions. Keep exploring!”

# Wrong-answer feedback rules

Wrong answers never end the game or remove points. WartsWorth gives one short hint and the player retries.

- **Biology hint:** “Look at the tooth’s shape and what that shape helps it do.”
- **Abstraction hint:** “We’re borrowing a function or strategy—not the whole snake.”
- **Engineering hint:** “Where would the clot contact the catheter during aspiration?”
- **Evidence hint:** “Does the evidence come from patients, or from testing before clinical use?”
- **Final-chain hint:** “Start with what exists in the organism, then follow the idea into engineering.”

The timer for the graded experience should measure the **required core path**, not optional time spent opening Sources/Transcript after Mission Complete. During implementation, time the game with a first-time tester. If the required path exceeds 5:00, shorten narration before removing biology, problem, technology, or course-connection content.
