# Phase 1 — Storyboard and Learning Design

## Target
A first-time player should finish in about **4–5 minutes**. The game uses narrator audio + matching captions, hover/focus descriptions, and meaningful click/choice interactions. Hover is supplemental; essential information is also available by focus/click/text.

## Scene 1 — Mission Briefing: The Blocked Vessel (35–45 sec)
**Setting:** hospital / simplified vessel view.

**Player does:** hovers/focuses on vessel, clot, and catheter; clicks the clot to begin the mission.

**Learns:** an ischemic stroke can occur when a clot blocks blood flow; thrombectomy aims to restore flow; clot retrieval is time-sensitive.

**Rubric:** Problem, Clarity.

**Narrative purpose:** present the problem before revealing the biological answer.

## Scene 2 — Retrieval Challenge (35–45 sec)
**Setting:** close-up of aspiration catheter approaching a clot.

**Player does:** tries a conventional aspiration retrieval. Animation shows suction drawing the clot toward the opening but establishes the retention challenge.

**Learns:** aspiration uses suction; smaller catheter diameter limits aspiration force, creating a need for better clot engagement/retention.

**Wrong-answer design:** choices do not block progress; narrator explains why “just use more suction/a larger tube” has constraints in small delicate vessels.

**Rubric:** Problem, Technology, Usefulness.

## Scene 3 — Nature Investigation: Boa Teeth (45–55 sec)
**Setting:** boa skull/jaw investigation.

**Player does:** explores tooth hotspots. Hover/focus gives short descriptions. Player identifies the feature most relevant to retaining soft tissue: recurved/inward-curving geometry.

**Learns:** boa teeth vary in curvature; curved teeth participate in prey capture/retention and swallowing. The relevant inspiration is geometry/function, not venom or sharpness alone.

**Rubric:** Biology, Creativity.

## Scene 4 — Mechanism Challenge: What Should We Borrow? (35–45 sec)
**Setting:** simplified tooth + soft-material diagram.

**Player does:** chooses among principles such as “copy the snake’s appearance,” “use venom,” or “use directional mechanical retention.”

**Correct concept:** directional mechanical retention from recurved structures.

**Fallback:** incorrect selections trigger a short explanation and allow another choice.

**Learns:** bioinspiration abstracts a functional principle rather than copying appearance.

**Rubric:** Biology, Course Connections, Creativity.

## Scene 5 — Engineering Lab: Meet TRAP (50–60 sec)
**Setting:** catheter cross-section / lab bench.

**Player does:** matches or drags the recurved biological feature to the inside of the catheter tip, then activates aspiration.

**Learns:** TRAP = Thrombus Retrieval Aspiration Platform; backward-curved microscale structures are inside the distal catheter tip; aspiration brings the clot into contact with them; the structures add mechanical engagement.

**Development note:** designed by Ángel Enríquez and Hyowon Lee; Purdue technology licensed to Emboa Medical.

**Rubric:** Technology, Connections.

## Scene 6 — Clot Capture Test (35–45 sec)
**Setting:** side-by-side simplified conventional smooth catheter and TRAP concept.

**Player does:** tests both approaches. The animation emphasizes mechanism, not a guaranteed clinical outcome.

**Learns:** TRAP was designed to increase extraction/retention force while keeping a slimmer catheter profile. Reported performance is preclinical/model evidence.

**Trade-off note:** device development still requires safety/effectiveness validation and manufacturable microstructures.

**Rubric:** Technology, Usefulness, Quality.

## Scene 7 — Bioinspiration Debrief (30–40 sec)
**Setting:** five-card chain.

**Player does:** arranges/selects the chain:
1. recurved boa teeth
2. retain/ensnare prey
3. directional mechanical retention
4. recurved catheter microstructures
5. improved clot engagement during aspiration

**Learns:** complete structure → function → abstraction → engineered feature → engineered function pathway.

**Course connection:** analogical reasoning, structure–function, abstraction, biology-to-engineering transfer, constraints/trade-offs.

**Rubric:** Connections, Biology, Technology, Usefulness.

## Scene 8 — Mission Complete / References (15–20 sec)
**Player does:** sees completion screen; can open Sources, Transcript, Replay.

**Learns:** concise final takeaway; technology is preclinical based on current sources.

**Rubric:** References, Accessibility, Clarity.

## Meaningful interactions
1. Investigate clot/problem via hover/focus + click.
2. Attempt conventional aspiration and learn its constraint.
3. Explore boa tooth hotspots and identify relevant geometry.
4. Choose the transferable biological principle.
5. Place/match the biological feature into the catheter design.
6. Test the concept.
7. Reconstruct the final bioinspiration chain.

## Estimated time
Narration/instruction: ~3:15–3:40.
Player exploration/choices: ~45–70 sec.
Target total: **4:00–4:50**.

## Rubric map
- **Biology:** Scenes 3, 4, 7
- **Problem:** Scenes 1, 2
- **Technology:** Scenes 5, 6, 7
- **Course Connections:** Scenes 4, 7
- **Length:** timed 4–5 min target
- **Creativity:** guided mission + scientific interactions
- **References:** Scene 8 + source cues
- **Accessibility:** captions, transcript, keyboard-equivalent focus/click
- **Clarity:** narrator + short hover definitions + progressive disclosure
- **Quality:** purposeful animations/interactions
- **Usefulness:** player must explain the complete analogy by the end
