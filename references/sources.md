# Phase 0 — Scientific Source Map

## Locked scientific story

**Biological structure → function → abstracted principle → engineered structure → engineered function**

Boa constrictor teeth are recurved/inward-curving, with tooth curvature varying across the jaws. Ryerson & Van Valkenburgh (2021) linked tooth shape with strike mechanics and observed curved posterior teeth ensnaring prey during some strikes. The transferable principle for this project is **directional mechanical retention**: a recurved structure can engage soft biological material and resist withdrawal.

Emboa Medical's **TRAP (Thrombus Retrieval Aspiration Platform)** applies that principle to aspiration thrombectomy. Recurved microscale structures are placed on the **inside of the distal catheter tip**. Aspiration draws a clot into the catheter opening; the microstructures engage the clot and add mechanical retention rather than relying on suction alone.

The game must not teach that the innovation is based on venom, tooth sharpness alone, or superficial resemblance.

## Medical problem

The target problem is ischemic stroke caused by an arterial obstruction. Aspiration catheters remove clots using suction, but aspiration force decreases as catheter diameter becomes smaller. Smaller, more flexible catheters are useful in delicate distal vessels, creating a design challenge: improve clot retention/extraction without simply increasing catheter size.

TRAP is a **preclinical/patent-pending technology** in the sources reviewed. Performance numbers reported by Purdue, Interventional News, and AskNature should be presented as bench/model results, not established clinical outcomes.

## Development history we can safely teach

- TRAP was designed by Ángel Enríquez and Hyowon Lee at Purdue.
- Purdue disclosed/protected the technology and licensed it to Emboa Medical for commercialization.
- In 2025 Purdue reported in-vitro/model validation and planned porcine testing.
- Do not state that TRAP is an approved standard-of-care device unless a later source establishes that.

### Peer-reviewed — thrombectomy context

## Reviewed but excluded from the core story

- PMC12390032: venom/coagulation topic; not evidence for boa tooth morphology or TRAP.
- PubMed PMID 28768818: SNAKE is “Sofia Non-wire Advancement techniKE”; unrelated to snake-tooth bioinspiration.

## Claim guardrails

- Say **recurved/inward-curving/backward-facing teeth** and explain retention; do not reduce the mechanism to “sharp teeth.”
- Separate biological evidence from engineering evidence.
- Describe reported TRAP performance as preclinical/model/bench results.
- Avoid claims of proven patient benefit, regulatory approval, or clinical superiority unless later evidence supports them.
